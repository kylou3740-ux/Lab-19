# NVIDIA --- Michael Burry Investor Memo

Verdict: PASS

## Screening results

NVIDIA passes the latest-year liabilities-to-equity check: the reported
ratio is 0.31, below the screen's maximum permitted level of twice equity. However, NVIDIA fails the
requirement that operating cash flow be at least as large as net income
in every reported fiscal year.

The five-year report shows that operating cash flow is below net income
in multiple years. Therefore, the all-years cash-flow check fails even
though operating cash flow exceeds net income in some of the reported
years.

## Interpretation

The balance-sheet screen is favorable, with reported liabilities modest
relative to equity under this exercise's threshold. The cash-flow result
is less favorable. A company may report substantial accounting earnings
while generating less operating cash in a given year; this screen flags
that difference for further investigation.

The failed check is not, by itself, proof of accounting problems or poor
business quality. Differences can arise from working-capital movements,
timing, and other operating items. The reason for the gap needs to be
understood from the filings.

## Limitations and missing information

This is a simplified screen using five fiscal years and a single
liabilities-to-equity comparison for the latest year. It does not
provide a full review of debt maturities, off-balance-sheet commitments,
working-capital drivers, or the quality and timing of revenue and
earnings. The cash-flow test is binary and does not explain why
operating cash flow fell below net income.

## Next research needed

Reconcile net income to operating cash flow for each year, paying
particular attention to working-capital changes, receivables, inventory,
and other non-cash adjustments. Review debt and liquidity disclosures,
revenue recognition policies, and the company's major customer and
supplier exposures. Reassess the conclusion only after understanding the
recurring versus temporary causes of the cash-flow gaps.
